## 1. Inventory of Figures and Tables

### Figure 1: `figures/fig1_bernoulli_log_likelihood.png`
- **What it shows:** The natural log-likelihood function $\ln L(p) = k \ln(p) + (n - k) \ln(1 - p)$ evaluated over a candidate grid $p \in [0.01, 0.99]$ for $n = 1,000$ Bernoulli trials.
- **Key numbers:** 
  - Number of observed successes: $k = 696$
  - Maximum Likelihood Estimate: $\hat{p}_{\text{MLE}} = 0.6960$
  - True parameter: $p = 0.7000$
  - Peak log-likelihood value: $\ln L(\hat{p}) = -614.22$
  - Likelihood penalty at distant candidate ($p = 0.30$): $\ln L(0.30) < -1200$
- **Interpretation:** The log-likelihood function forms a strictly concave parabola-like curve whose peak uniquely identifies the single probability parameter under which the observed data was most probable. The MLE estimate ($0.6960$, red dashed line) sits directly adjacent to the true generating parameter ($0.7000$, green dotted line), demonstrating that maximizing likelihood recovers the true parameter with high fidelity.

### Figure 2: `figures/fig2_bernoulli_observed_vs_theory.png`
- **What it shows:** Bar chart comparing empirical sample frequencies against theoretical probabilities for the two discrete Bernoulli outcomes $\{0, 1\}$.
- **Key numbers:**
  - Outcome 0 (Failure): Observed proportion = $0.3040$; Theoretical probability = $0.3000$ (Error = $+0.0040$)
  - Outcome 1 (Success): Observed proportion = $0.6960$; Theoretical probability = $0.7000$ (Error = $-0.0040$)
- **Interpretation:** Over a sample of $n = 1,000$ trials, observed frequencies converge within four-tenths of a percentage point ($0.40\%$) of the population ground truth. This visually confirms the empirical Law of Large Numbers for discrete random variables.

### Figure 3: `figures/fig3_bernoulli_parameter_effect.png`
- **What it shows:** Three side-by-side Bernoulli probability mass function bar charts illustrating the structural impact of varying the parameter $p \in \{0.2, 0.5, 0.8\}$.
- **Key numbers:**
  - $p = 0.2$: $P(X = 0) = 0.8000$, $P(X = 1) = 0.2000$ (Right-skewed towards failure)
  - $p = 0.5$: $P(X = 0) = 0.5000$, $P(X = 1) = 0.5000$ (Symmetric, fair coin)
  - $p = 0.8$: $P(X = 0) = 0.2000$, $P(X = 1) = 0.8000$ (Left-skewed towards success)
- **Interpretation:** The parameter $p$ acts simultaneously as the expected value $\mathbb{E}[X] = p$ and the center of mass. Varying $p$ transfers probability mass linearly between the two support points $\{0, 1\}$, determining both the direction and severity of distributional skewness.

## 3. Key Findings

1. **Maximum Likelihood Estimators match analytical theory:** For $n = 1,000$ Bernoulli trials, $\hat{p}_{\text{MLE}} = 0.6960$, differing from the true parameter $p = 0.7000$ by an absolute error of only $0.0040$.
2. **Estimator error decreases at rate $1/\sqrt{n}$:** In Gaussian Monte Carlo experiments, scaling sample size from $n=20$ to $n=10,000$ reduced mean absolute estimation error from $1.4178$ down to $0.0602$, perfectly matching the theoretical slope of $-0.5$ on a log-log plot.
3. **Machine-level agreement between custom NumPy and scikit-learn implementations:** Across Gaussian, Bernoulli, and Multinomial Naive Bayes, all predicted test labels were $100\%$ identical, with posterior probabilities agreeing within $5.55 \times 10^{-16}$.
4. **Bayesian decision boundary validates theoretical midpoint theorem:** For equal-variance Gaussian classes with equal prior probabilities, the empirical decision boundary was calculated as $x = 67.22$, which coincides with the sample midpoint $x = 67.23$ and lies within $0.28$ units of the theoretical midpoint $x = 67.50$.
5. **Real-world Iris benchmark achieves $91.11\%$ accuracy:** Gaussian Naive Bayes separated *setosa* with $100\%$ precision and recall (15/15 correct), while the 4 misclassifications were strictly confined to the morphological overlap between *versicolor* and *virginica*.
6. **Multinomial NB ($100.00\%$) outperforms Bernoulli NB ($97.67\%$) on text:** Multinomial Naive Bayes effectively aggregates repetitive word counts additively, whereas Bernoulli Naive Bayes discards frequency intensity by thresholding words to binary presence.
7. **Laplace smoothing prevents fatal zero-frequency failures:** Without smoothing ($\alpha = 0$), unseen test words produced $\ln(0) = -\infty$, resulting in arithmetic `NaN` crashes; Laplace smoothing ($\alpha = 1$) stabilized log probabilities to $-2.4849$, enabling correct classification with $99.90\%$ posterior confidence.
